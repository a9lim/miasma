# Miasma

Miasma is a spatial epidemic simulator on a hex grid. A hantavirus outbreak in my corner of California prompted it: I wanted to actually feel the dynamics of an epidemic instead of just reading the curves in a paper. The textbook SEIR model is a system of ODEs that smears every person into one well-mixed pool; that's mathematically clean, but it loses everything that makes a real outbreak feel like an outbreak: the wavefront, the holes in the wavefront, the way a quarantine ring either catches the wave or doesn't, the way a vaccine campaign that's a week too late looks indistinguishable from one that's on time until it suddenly doesn't.

Hex grids give you isotropic diffusion. Six neighbors, no rook-vs-bishop asymmetry, no jagged wavefronts pretending to be smooth. Every occupied cell carries one of eight compartments (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered, deceased, vaccinated, maternal-immunity, or Z), an age that increments every tick, a health value that degrades during infection, and up to four simultaneous strain loads that sum to one. Z is structurally present but disabled by default; the Zombie apocalypse and Immunoediting presets enable it. Strains can mutate and recombine, and each sits at a place in antigenic space: a cell remembers the antigens it has met, and its protection against a strain falls off with the strain's antigenic distance from them. Newborns of recovered parents enter with maternal antibodies that decay back to susceptible over a window, deceased cells can be disposed into empty occupancy, and empty cells repopulate from neighbor density. The result is a fixed spatial lattice with stochastic compartment and occupancy dynamics.

An optional animal reservoir runs its own SIR layer on the same hex grid, orthogonal to the human compartments. It carries the same demography the human population does — animals age, breed into empty cells from inhabited neighbors, and die of age-ramped natural mortality on top of disease — and its transmission, recovery, and mortality rates are per-strain: an infectious animal carries a strain id, and animal-to-animal spread reads that strain's reservoir β/γ/μ off the registry. Spillover is bidirectional and strain-aware: an infectious animal hands its own strain to a co-located susceptible human, and an infectious human hands its primary strain back to a co-located susceptible animal. Hantavirus and plague presets lean on this; vanilla SEIR turns the reservoir off entirely.

Three flags overlay extra states on the base compartments. Latent (L) marks an exposed cell whose progression to infectious goes through a slow reactivation rate instead of the normal incubation σ — a fraction of fresh exposures latch latent on contact, configurable per strain. Chronic-carrier (C) marks a recovered cell that still transmits at a fraction of the baseline β — useful for hantavirus-style reservoir dynamics where some recoveries don't clear. Infectious-corpse (F) marks a deceased cell whose body is still a transmission source until it clears, and is the gate that lets D→Z conversion happen in zombie mode. Recovered and vaccinated cells can be infected again by strains that have drifted from what they remember: an infection protects against its own antigen at a set strength, a vaccine against its antigen at its efficacy, and both fall off with antigenic distance, so reinfections and breakthroughs favor drifted strains. A vaccine is matched to a strain, so it loses ground as the virus drifts away from it. The Threshold preset pins immunity to the textbook shape; the sandbox keeps partial reinfection and breakthrough live.

The Z compartment is interpretation-agnostic. The state machine is one set of transitions; two readings emerge depending on parameter values and which spawn pathway is active. Macroscopic (zombie pandemic): the F-corpse pathway is on, infectious-deceased cells reanimate at dz_dead per tick, encounters between zombies and humans roll a single bucketed outcome across clean kill, kill-with-infection (sets F on the corpse), expose alive (target → E), or unopposed conversion (target → Z), with a separate roll for the zombie losing the fight (z_die_fighting); zombies have a finite lifespan via z_die_natural. Microscopic (oncoviral cellular transformation): cells, not people. The L-flag pathway is on, latent integration of an oncovirus into the host genome occasionally triggers malignant transformation at l_transform per tick, transformed cells expand clonally through z_convert_unopposed (the "fight" outcomes go to zero because cells don't fight), immune surveillance is represented by a small z_die_fighting rate (NK and cytotoxic T cells clearing transformed cells), transformed cells are immortal so z_die_natural is zero, and crowding-driven exhaustion stays on to capture tumor-core necrosis from hypoxia. Two presets ship the calibrated configurations: `zombie-apocalypse` and `oncoviral-transformation`. Intermediate parameter values produce intermediate readings — partial immune fight-back, weak clonal expansion, hybrid pathways — which is sometimes the most useful thing to play with.

## What you can do with it

You can pick one of six experiments, each with one knob and a number measured live in the figure caption: Threshold (how far above the critical transmission rate an outbreak has to be before it spreads, and how ragged it is just above), Herd immunity (how much random vaccine coverage stops a wave when people only meet their neighbours: about half, not the textbook 82%), Antigenic drift (seasonal waves of variants that drift away from what hosts remember), Spillover (human chains too weak to last, kept going by a rodent reservoir), Immunoediting (immune surveillance eliminating, holding or losing a tumour, read as an H&E section), and Zombie apocalypse (the living fighting back). Or seed patient zero by clicking, and build your own pathogen in the Sandbox from the parameter sliders, watching observed Re, incubation period, infectious window, and mortality interact. The surface picker glues the edges of the hexagonal world into a plane, torus, Klein bottle, or RP², the same way Worldline does for continuous geometry but on a discrete hex lattice. The Klein bottle and RP² produce genuinely strange epidemic dynamics, the kind that don't appear in any real-world model because real epidemics don't run on non-orientable manifolds.

Interventions are a paint brush. Pick vaccinate, quarantine, sanitize, or cull, drag across the grid, watch the wavefront react. You can undo paint strokes until the simulation advances; once time moves, the undo stack is cleared so intervention history never rewinds the disease state. There's also a rollout slider for auto-vaccinate: turn it on and a campaign runs in the background, converting susceptible, exposed, recovered, and maternal-immune cells to vaccinated at a configurable rate per tick. The "did we roll out fast enough" question is the one you actually want to be able to feel, and that's what the slider is for. Nightingale's rose sits at the top of the Counts tab and counts exposures, recoveries, deaths, and new zombies or transformed cells in ten-day wedges, with the animal-reservoir census listed below the human compartment readouts. The Strains tab opens on an antigenic map, every strain at its place on an antigenic torus, which is also the key to how the plate hatches strains: angle and colour are position. Below it are a clickable time-scaled phylogeny showing point mutations and recombination events, the top eight strains by prevalence, a button that matches the vaccine to the selected strain, and a per-strain genome editor that includes the reservoir β/γ/μ alongside the human SEIR rates.

The canvas zooms and pans. Wheel zooms toward the cursor, two-finger pinch on touch devices, plus dedicated +/−/reset buttons and a percentage readout — useful for inspecting the texture of a wavefront when the fit-to-canvas view makes individual hexes too small to read comfortably.

Three intents, really. The textbook SEIR view is for learning: see Kermack-McKendrick come to life, see why reproduction number matters, see what flattening the curve actually looks like as a spatial process. The dual-reading Z view is for seeing one state machine become two different stories: the same compartment runs a zombie pandemic and an oncoviral tumor growth depending on which preset or parameters turn Z on. The Sandbox is for play: turn on zombies, ramp up the mutation rate, give the rats Ebola, see what happens.

## Accessibility

Miasma's colophon, experiment strip, margin tabs, surface picker, selectors, sliders, and documented paint-mode shortcuts are keyboard-operable. The main canvas has an accessible label, but placing brush strokes on particular cells still requires a pointer or touch; phylogeny and antigenic-map selection are also pointer-driven, though the strain rows below them select the same strains. Toolbar actions and generated controls carry ARIA labels, while compartment counts, status counts, the animal census, and observed Re render as text alongside the map, and the live figure caption under the plate states the current counts. The theme toggle switches between light and dark presentations. Known hazards: rapid changes across the hex grid during fast-forward playback may be uncomfortable for some users. Please pause the sim or drop playback speed if needed; with reduced motion set, the sim opens paused on a finished plate. The strain panel pairs its phylogeny with text rows and a detail inspector, so parentage and genome values remain readable without selecting nodes on the canvas.
