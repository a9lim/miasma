# Miasma

Miasma is a spatial epidemic simulator on a hex grid. I built it because there's a hantavirus outbreak happening in my corner of California right now, and I wanted to actually feel the dynamics of an epidemic instead of just reading the curves in a paper. The textbook SEIR model is a system of ODEs that smears every person into one well-mixed pool; that's mathematically clean, but it loses everything that makes a real outbreak feel like an outbreak: the wavefront, the holes in the wavefront, the way a quarantine ring either catches the wave or doesn't, the way a vaccine campaign that's a week too late looks indistinguishable from one that's on time until it suddenly doesn't.

Hex grids give you isotropic diffusion. Six neighbors, no rook-vs-bishop asymmetry, no jagged wavefronts pretending to be smooth. Every cell carries a compartment (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered, deceased, vaccinated, maternal-immunity, plus an optional zombie compartment for the absurd preset), an age that increments every tick, a health value that degrades with each prior infection, and up to four simultaneous strain loads that sum to one. Strains can mutate, recombine, and cross-react with prior exposure through a similarity matrix. Newborns enter with maternal antibodies that decay back to susceptible over a window. Empty cells repopulate from neighbor density. The whole thing is a closed-system population dynamics model wrapped around a stochastic compartmental core.

## What you can do with it

You can pick a preset (vanilla SEIR, COVID-19, Ebola, TB, Andes hantavirus, smallpox, plague with a rat reservoir, or the absurd-mode-everything-on preset which turns on zombies and every secondary mechanic at once), seed patient zero by clicking, and watch the curves. Or you can build your own pathogen from a parameter slider and see how R0, incubation period, infectious window, and mortality interact. Topology toggle lets you wrap the grid as a plane, cylinder, torus, möbius strip, klein bottle, or RP², the same way the geon sim does for continuous geometry but on a discrete hex lattice. Möbius and klein topologies produce genuinely strange epidemic dynamics, the kind that don't appear in any real-world model because real epidemics don't run on non-orientable manifolds.

Interventions are a gerry-style brush. Pick vaccinate, quarantine, sanitize, or cull, drag across the grid, watch the wavefront react. You can undo. Time-series panel at the bottom tracks every compartment count, switchable between stacked-area and line-overlay views. Strain registry panel on the side surfaces the top eight strains by prevalence with a clickable lineage tree showing point mutations and recombination events.

Two intents, really. The textbook SEIR view is for learning: see Kermack-McKendrick come to life, see why R0 matters, see what flattening the curve actually looks like as a spatial process. The absurd mode is for play: turn on zombies, ramp up the mutation rate, give the rats Ebola, see what happens.

## Accessibility

Miasma supports keyboard navigation for all controls, high-contrast mode via the theme toggle, and ARIA labels on toolbar buttons and compartment readouts. Compartment counts render as numerical values alongside the colored hex map, so colorblind users can read the state directly off the sidebar. Known hazards: rapid color changes across the hex grid during fast-forward playback may be uncomfortable for some users. Please pause the sim or drop playback speed if needed. The strain lineage tree has a text-mode fallback that lists parent-child relationships as a nested list.
