# Miasma

Miasma is a spatial epidemic simulator on a hex grid. I built it because there's a hantavirus outbreak happening in my corner of California right now, and I wanted to actually feel the dynamics of an epidemic instead of just reading the curves in a paper. The textbook SEIR model is a system of ODEs that smears every person into one well-mixed pool; that's mathematically clean, but it loses everything that makes a real outbreak feel like an outbreak: the wavefront, the holes in the wavefront, the way a quarantine ring either catches the wave or doesn't, the way a vaccine campaign that's a week too late looks indistinguishable from one that's on time until it suddenly doesn't.

Hex grids give you isotropic diffusion. Six neighbors, no rook-vs-bishop asymmetry, no jagged wavefronts pretending to be smooth. Every cell carries a compartment (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered, deceased, vaccinated, maternal-immunity, plus an optional zombie compartment for the absurd preset), an age that increments every tick, a health value that degrades during infection, and up to four simultaneous strain loads that sum to one. Strains can mutate, recombine, and cross-react with prior exposure through a per-cell bloom-filter record. Newborns enter with maternal antibodies that decay back to susceptible over a window. Empty cells repopulate from neighbor density. The whole thing is a closed-system population dynamics model wrapped around a stochastic compartmental core.

An optional animal reservoir runs its own SIR layer on the same hex grid, orthogonal to the human compartments. It carries the same demography the human population does — animals age, breed into empty cells from inhabited neighbors, and die of age-ramped natural mortality on top of disease — and its transmission, recovery, and mortality rates are per-strain: an infectious animal carries a strain id, and animal-to-animal spread reads that strain's reservoir β/γ/μ off the registry. Spillover is bidirectional and strain-aware: an infectious animal hands its own strain to a co-located susceptible human, and an infectious human hands its primary strain back to a co-located susceptible animal. Hantavirus and plague presets lean on this; vanilla SEIR turns the reservoir off entirely.

Three flags overlay extra states on the base compartments. Latent (L) marks an exposed cell whose progression to infectious goes through a slow reactivation rate instead of the normal incubation σ — a fraction of fresh exposures latch latent on contact, configurable per preset (TB sets this near 0.9). Chronic-carrier (C) marks a recovered cell that still transmits at a fraction of the baseline β — useful for hantavirus-style reservoir dynamics where some recoveries don't clear. Infectious-corpse (F) marks a deceased cell whose body is still a transmission source until it clears, and is the gate that lets D→Z conversion happen in zombie mode. Recovered cells can be reinfected when a per-preset susceptibility multiplier is positive: the per-strain bloom-filter cross-immunity already scales contributions from previously-seen strains, so reinfections favor novel antigens — the right shape for waning population-level immunity. Vaccinated cells get a separate efficacy parameter that admits breakthrough infections at the residual rate when efficacy is below one. The vanilla preset pins immunity back to the textbook shape; the default sandbox keeps partial reinfection and breakthrough live.

The Z compartment is interpretation-agnostic. The state machine is one set of transitions; two readings emerge depending on parameter values and which spawn pathway is active. Macroscopic (zombie pandemic): the F-corpse pathway is on, infectious-deceased cells reanimate at dz_dead per tick, encounters between zombies and humans roll a single bucketed outcome across clean kill, kill-with-infection (sets F on the corpse), expose alive (target → E), or unopposed conversion (target → Z), with a separate roll for the zombie losing the fight (z_die_fighting); zombies have a finite lifespan via z_die_natural. Microscopic (oncoviral cellular transformation): cells, not people. The L-flag pathway is on, latent integration of an oncovirus into the host genome occasionally triggers malignant transformation at l_transform per tick, transformed cells expand clonally through z_convert_unopposed (the "fight" outcomes go to zero because cells don't fight), immune surveillance is represented by a small z_die_fighting rate (NK and cytotoxic T cells clearing transformed cells), transformed cells are immortal so z_die_natural is zero, and crowding-driven exhaustion stays on to capture tumor-core necrosis from hypoxia. Two presets ship the calibrated configurations: `zombie-apocalypse` and `oncoviral-transformation`. Intermediate parameter values produce intermediate readings — partial immune fight-back, weak clonal expansion, hybrid pathways — which is sometimes the most useful thing to play with.

## What you can do with it

You can pick a preset (vanilla SEIR, COVID-19, Ebola, TB, Andes hantavirus, smallpox, plague with a rat reservoir, zombie apocalypse with humans fighting back, oncoviral cellular transformation, or the absurd-mode-everything-on preset which turns on zombies and every secondary mechanic at once), seed patient zero by clicking, and watch the curves. Or you can build your own pathogen from the parameter sliders and watch observed Re, incubation period, infectious window, and mortality interact. The Settings tab lets you wrap the grid as a plane, cylinder, torus, möbius strip, klein bottle, or RP², the same way the geon sim does for continuous geometry but on a discrete hex lattice. Möbius and klein topologies produce genuinely strange epidemic dynamics, the kind that don't appear in any real-world model because real epidemics don't run on non-orientable manifolds.

Interventions are a gerry-style brush. Pick vaccinate, quarantine, sanitize, or cull, drag across the grid, watch the wavefront react. You can undo paint strokes until the simulation advances; once time moves, the undo stack is cleared so intervention history never rewinds the disease state. There's also a rollout slider for auto-vaccinate: turn it on and a campaign runs in the background, converting susceptible, exposed, recovered, and maternal-immune cells to vaccinated at a configurable rate per tick. The "did we roll out fast enough" question is the one you actually want to be able to feel, and that's what the slider is for. The time-series panel sits at the top of the Compartments tab and tracks every compartment count as a stacked-area chart, with the animal-reservoir census listed below the human compartment readouts. Strain registry panel on the side surfaces the top eight strains by prevalence with a clickable lineage forest showing point mutations and recombination events, and a per-strain genome editor that now includes the reservoir β/γ/μ alongside the human SEIR rates.

The canvas zooms and pans. Wheel zooms toward the cursor, two-finger pinch on touch devices, plus dedicated +/−/reset buttons and a percentage readout — useful for inspecting the texture of a wavefront when the fit-to-canvas view makes individual hexes too small to read comfortably.

Three intents, really. The textbook SEIR view is for learning: see Kermack-McKendrick come to life, see why reproduction number matters, see what flattening the curve actually looks like as a spatial process. The dual-reading Z view is for seeing one state machine become two different stories: the same compartment runs a zombie pandemic and an oncoviral tumor growth depending on which preset or parameters turn Z on. The absurd mode is for play: turn on zombies, ramp up the mutation rate, give the rats Ebola, see what happens.

## Accessibility

Miasma supports keyboard navigation for all controls, high-contrast mode via the theme toggle, and ARIA labels on toolbar buttons and compartment readouts. Compartment counts render as numerical values alongside the colored hex map, so colorblind users can read the state directly off the sidebar. Known hazards: rapid color changes across the hex grid during fast-forward playback may be uncomfortable for some users. Please pause the sim or drop playback speed if needed. The strain panel pairs the lineage forest with text rows and a detail inspector, so parentage and genome values remain readable without relying on the canvas alone.
